# TecnoAyuda — Business Analysis

## Case 29

This project is an analysis of **TecnoAyuda**, an IT support and maintenance company that provides services to small and medium-sized businesses in the local area.

The analysis follows the structure requested in the assignment: organization, diagnosis, ERP/CRM proposal, architecture, B2B/B2C/B2E workflows, and conclusion.

## 1. Company Overview

TecnoAyuda provides computer support and maintenance services to small and medium-sized companies.

The company has **14 technicians** who provide both on-site and remote technical support.

Customers currently report problems through different communication channels:

- Phone calls
- Email
- WhatsApp

This situation makes incident management difficult because there is no centralized system.

## 2. Organization

The company can be organized into several main areas:

- **Management:** Responsible for the general administration and decision-making of the company.
- **Technical Support:** Technicians who solve customer incidents, both remotely and on-site.
- **Customer Management:** Responsible for maintaining customer information, contracts and communication.
- **Administration:** Responsible for invoices, contracts and other administrative tasks.

The technical support department is the most important operational area because it manages and resolves customer incidents.

## 3. Diagnosis

The main problem identified in TecnoAyuda is the lack of a centralized incident management system.

Customers can contact the company through different channels, but these incidents are not managed in a single platform.

This can cause several problems:

- Two technicians may be assigned to the same incident.
- Other customers may have to wait for a response for several hours.
- It is difficult to know the current status of an incident.
- Customer information is not centralized.
- It is difficult to monitor technician workload.
- Customers cannot directly check the status of their requests.

The company therefore needs a digital solution that centralizes incidents and improves communication between customers and technicians.

## 4. ERP / CRM Proposal

The proposed solution is to implement an integrated **ERP and CRM system**.

The **CRM** would be mainly used to manage customers, contracts and support incidents.

The system could include:

- Customer database
- Customer contracts
- Incident management
- Technician assignment
- Incident status tracking
- Communication history
- Customer portal
- Reports and statistics

The ERP component could help with administrative processes such as contracts, invoices and company resources.

The most important feature would be a centralized **ticketing system**.

Each customer request would create a ticket containing information such as:

- Customer
- Problem description
- Date and time
- Priority
- Assigned technician
- Current status
- Resolution

## 5. Proposed Architecture

The proposed architecture would be based on a centralized cloud system.

The main components would be:

**Customers → Customer Portal → CRM / Ticketing System → Technicians**

Customers would be able to access the portal and create new incidents.

The CRM would receive and organize the tickets. The system could then assign incidents to available technicians according to factors such as workload, location and specialization.

Technicians would access the platform to view their assigned incidents and update their status.

The system would also store all information in a centralized database.

## 6. B2B Workflow

TecnoAyuda mainly operates through a **B2B (Business-to-Business)** model because its customers are other companies.

A typical B2B workflow would be:

1. A customer company has a technical problem.
2. An authorized employee accesses the customer portal.
3. The employee creates a support ticket.
4. The system registers the incident.
5. The incident is assigned to a technician.
6. The technician solves the problem remotely or visits the customer.
7. The technician updates the ticket.
8. The customer can see the progress through the portal.
9. The incident is closed when the problem is solved.

This workflow would reduce duplicated assignments and improve response times.

## 7. B2C Workflow

B2C is not the main business model of TecnoAyuda because the company provides services mainly to small and medium-sized businesses.

However, if TecnoAyuda decided to offer support services directly to individual customers in the future, the same CRM and ticketing system could be adapted for individual users.

Customers could create accounts, report incidents and check the status of their requests through the same type of portal.

## 8. B2E Workflow

The **B2E (Business-to-Employee)** workflow would focus on the 14 technicians and other employees of TecnoAyuda.

Employees could use the internal system to:

- View assigned incidents.
- Check their workload.
- Update ticket status.
- Add technical notes.
- Record solutions.
- Consult customer information.
- View their schedules and tasks.

This would give employees a centralized workspace and make the distribution of technical work more organized.

## 9. Conclusion

TecnoAyuda's main challenge is the decentralized management of technical support requests.

The use of different communication channels such as phone, email and WhatsApp can cause duplicated work and delays.

The proposed ERP/CRM solution, together with a centralized ticketing system and a customer portal, would allow TecnoAyuda to organize its incidents, technicians and customer information in a single platform.

The solution would improve both the internal management of the company and the service provided to its business customers.
